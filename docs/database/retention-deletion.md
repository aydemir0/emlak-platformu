# Data retention and deletion

## Purpose

Define the lifecycle contract for soft deletion, restoration, privacy erasure, retention, and hard purge across the canonical data model. Retention durations are configuration, not hard-coded business constants. Numeric periods are deliberately not invented; product, legal, regulatory, backup, SEO, and operational owners must approve them before implementation. Authorization follows the [authorization matrix](authorization-matrix.md) and [RLS design](rls-design.md).

## Data classification

| Class | Examples in the canonical tables | Handling intention |
|---|---|---|
| Public business data | Published subsets of `properties`, `locations`, `advisors`, catalog vocabularies, `seo_pages`, `content_entries`, route resolution backed by `public_route_reservations`, and eligible `property_media` variants | Public only while current publication and non-deleted rules pass; removal from public delivery may be faster than storage purge. |
| Operational confidential data | Draft property/content facts, assignments, prices/history, upload/processing state, SEO query definitions, site settings, outbox state | Staff/service access by purpose; never placed in public caches or analytics. |
| Direct PII | `user_identities`, advisor private fields, `leads`, `customers`, `customer_contact_points`, appointment participant/contact fields | Highest minimization, access, export, retention, erasure, and incident controls; prohibited from URLs, GA4, cache keys, ordinary logs, and outbox payloads. |
| Linked/pseudonymous personal data | `lead_conversions`, `customer_merge_history`, `customer_requests`, `customer_activities`, `appointments`, property/customer matches and reasons, identifiers in audit/analytics | Personal data whenever linkable. Minimize details, separate identifiers from content where feasible, and include in access/export/erasure analysis. |
| Sensitive security/audit data | `roles`, `permissions`, assignments, `audit_logs`, authorization denials, identity/offboarding evidence | Restricted by job purpose, append-only for normal roles, and retained under a separately approved evidence policy. Do not retain secrets, tokens, or unnecessary before/after PII. |
| PII-free telemetry | Approved `analytics_events` and definitions | Separate purpose/consent/retention boundary. Events must remain free of direct PII; linkability and deletion obligations are reviewed before collection. |
| Media and embedded metadata | Quarantine/original objects represented by upload/media rows, public variants, EXIF/GPS before stripping | Treat uploads as untrusted and potentially personal. Originals remain private; public variants contain stripped metadata. Database and R2 lifecycles are reconciled. |

Classification follows content, not only table name. Free text, JSON, error details, history snapshots, audit summaries, and outbox payloads can become PII and therefore use allowlisted schemas and redaction.

## Universal lifecycle rules

- Business records are soft-deleted when recoverability is required. `deleted_at is null` is the default active predicate; sensitive deletions also record actor, reason, and correlation/audit evidence without copying unnecessary PII.
- Delete and restore are `ADMIN`-only application use cases in V1, not ad hoc updates. They re-check current permission, state, references, uniqueness, and child behavior in one defined transaction boundary. Advisor end, detach, cancel, dismiss, and deactivate actions are separate lifecycle commands, not delete/restore authority.
- Soft deletion immediately removes the record from public/admin ordinary reads, shared caches, sitemaps, matches, assignments, and delivery eligibility as applicable. It is not privacy erasure.
- Normal application actors cannot hard-delete business records. A privileged, bounded, idempotent purge verifies retention expiry, reference rules, erasure status, backup policy, and legal hold, then records non-sensitive evidence.
- Append-only history, audit, analytics, and outbox rows are not casually soft-deleted. They follow their own minimized retention/purge policies and never preserve forbidden PII merely because they are evidence.
- Foreign-key cascades are not assumed. Each family below declares whether children hide, detach, independently soft-delete, remain as minimized history, or purge with the parent.
- Every configurable retention value has a documented owner, data class, environment-independent meaning, effective date/version, minimum/maximum validation, audit trail, and safe fallback. Configuration cannot shorten a legal hold or silently override an applicable legal minimum.

## Soft-delete uniqueness and restore contract

Business identities that may be reused after deletion use active-row uniqueness, conceptually equivalent to uniqueness among rows whose `deleted_at` is absent. Identities that must never be reused—immutable public IDs, provider identity bindings where account takeover is possible, idempotency keys, and retained historical slugs where redirects must remain unambiguous—remain non-reusable under an explicit rule.

A restore never silently wins a uniqueness conflict. The use case reports the conflicting active record and requires a permitted resolution: keep the deleted record deleted, choose a new business value, merge through the relevant audited workflow, or retire the conflicting active record when independently authorized. Relationships are revalidated against current active parents; stale assignments, roles, contacts, media eligibility, matches, or SEO links are not silently resurrected.

RLS and all ordinary repository queries hide soft-deleted rows from anonymous users, customers, and advisors. Admins see deleted rows only through an explicit recovery/retention permission and purpose-built query. Privileged services see them only for named purge, restore, reconciliation, incident, or legal tasks. Public caches and R2 delivery must deny deleted content even if a stale object URL exists.

## Soft-deletable table families

The following table enumerates every family intentionally modeled as soft-deletable in this design. Tables not listed are immutable history/event rows, pure detach junctions, or reference data that must use an explicit deprecation/versioning decision before soft deletion is introduced.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity/access: `user_identities`, `advisors`, `roles`, `permissions` | Provider identity bindings, advisor identity links, role codes, and permission codes remain permanently unique to prevent takeover or historical reinterpretation. Property assignments end independently rather than cascade-delete properties. | Restore requires provider-link availability, active account status, current invitation/offboarding approval, and conflict checks for handles/contact values. Roles/permissions are not restored into assignments automatically. | Purge direct identity/profile PII after the approved window or valid erasure request while retaining minimum non-PII audit evidence. Legal hold can defer destruction but cannot restore access. Backups follow delayed expiry and access restriction. |

New assignments are denied. | Restore checks whether meaning remains identical; semantic replacement uses a new code/record rather than rewriting history. | Purge only when unreferenced and no reporting/SEO interpretation requires it. No expected PII, but backups/holds still follow integrity policy. |
| Property/location: `properties`, `locations` | Active property slug/business keys and location slugs use conflict-aware active uniqueness while immutable IDs/public IDs and retained slug history remain non-reusable. Secrets never belong here. Backups and holds follow configuration recovery policy without reviving retired settings. |

### Tables deliberately not soft-deletable by default

- Append-only histories/evidence and globally reserved routes: `property_state_history`, `property_slug_history`, `property_price_history`, `lead_conversions`, `customer_merge_history`, `seo_page_slug_history`, `content_slug_history`, `location_slug_history`, `public_route_reservations`, `analytics_events`, and `audit_logs`. Retired route reservations remain reserved and are not ordinarily soft-deleted for reuse.
- Pure or derived junctions detached/rebuilt through parent commands: `role_permissions`, `property_feature_assignments`, `customer_request_features`, `property_customer_match_reasons`, and `seo_page_features`.
- Explicitly ended/terminal operational records: `user_role_assignments`, `property_advisor_assignments`, `media_upload_sessions`, and append-only `customer_activities`.
- Worker/infrastructure records with bounded terminal retention: `property_media_variants`, `media_processing_attempts`, and `outbox_messages`.

These records still require a hard-purge/anonymization schedule. “Append-only” means normal roles cannot update/delete; it does not override privacy law or an approved exceptional purge.

## Privacy erasure workflow

1. Authenticate and authorize the requester/administrator without revealing whether unrelated identities exist; record the request and lawful scope.
2. Discover direct and linkable PII across identity, lead, customer, contact, request, activity, appointment, match, media metadata/objects, audit summaries, analytics identifiers, outbox payloads, exports, vendor systems, and backups.
3. Apply legal hold, fraud/security, contractual, and statutory retention decisions per data element. A hold preserves only what is necessary and removes ordinary operational visibility.
4. Immediately restrict processing/public delivery, revoke access links, and stop new downstream emission.
5. Purge or irreversibly anonymize eligible data in dependency order. Preserve only minimized non-identifying evidence where a documented basis requires it; never keep a reversible lookup merely labeled anonymized.
6. Propagate erasure/tombstones to caches, R2, analytics providers, email/export destinations, and other processors; retry and reconcile idempotently.
7. Record safe completion evidence and unresolved held items without copying erased values. If a backup is restored, replay erasure tombstones before returning the environment to service.

Audit and analytics require special handling. Audit evidence is append-only to normal roles but must use safe summaries and may have PII redacted/anonymized through an exceptional controlled process. Analytics remains separate from operational/audit data, accepts no direct PII, and deletes linkable subject identifiers under its approved consent/retention policy.

## Backups and legal hold

- Define encrypted backup retention, access, restore testing, residency, and destruction independently from live-table retention. Soft deletion does not remove backup copies immediately.
- Erasure responses disclose the backup delay where legally required. Tombstones or an equivalent replayable erasure ledger prevent restored backups from resurrecting erased identities or public content.
- A legal hold is scoped, authorized, time-bounded/reviewed, and audited. It pauses eligible purge but never restores public, customer, advisor, or ordinary admin visibility.
- Exports, replicas, staging copies, logs, observability, vendor stores, and R2 objects are part of the lifecycle inventory; production PII is not copied to non-production by default.

## Purge verification requirements

- Test each soft-deletable family for ordinary invisibility, explicit recovery access, public/cache/media revocation, uniqueness reuse, restore conflict, child handling, hard purge, erasure, legal hold, and backup/tombstone restoration behavior.
- Test idempotent retry and partial failure across PostgreSQL, R2, CDN/cache, analytics, email/export, and other processors.
- Reconciliation reports identify overdue rows, held rows, missing objects, orphan objects, stale public references, and failed downstream erasure without including raw PII.
- Purge tools use bounded batches, least privilege, correlation IDs, safe dry-run/count review, and audit evidence. They cannot accept arbitrary table names or caller-selected authorization scope.

## Assumptions

- Exact legal regimes and numeric retention periods are not yet confirmed.
- Soft deletion is the default for recoverable business records; immutable evidence and worker data use separate terminal retention.
- PostgreSQL is authoritative for lifecycle state, while R2 is authoritative only for media bytes.
- Public removal, operational deletion, privacy erasure, and physical backup expiry are separate milestones.

## Open Decisions

- Legal jurisdictions, lawful bases, consent rules, subject-access/export scope, and exact live/backup/vendor retention periods per data class.
- Which business identifiers can be reused after soft deletion and which remain permanently reserved.
- Detailed property/location child behavior, SEO outcome, and media retention after property deletion.
- Whether any audit/history facts must be retained, anonymized, or exceptionally erased under applicable law.
- V2 customer-account identity-linking, recovery, and erasure workflow; V1 has no customer/public account.
- Legal-hold authority, review cadence, release procedure, and how holds are represented without broadening access.
- Backup provider topology, recovery objectives, tombstone replay mechanism, and proof of final destruction.
